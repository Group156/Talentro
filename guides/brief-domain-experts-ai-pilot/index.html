AI Implementation: How to Brief Domain Experts for a Pilot

An AI implementation pilot needs a clear answer to a practical question: what must the system do well enough for the team to use it? Domain experts can help evaluate subject-matter accuracy, but useful feedback starts with a specific review brief.

Define the decision the pilot should support

Write down the intended user, workflow and output. A reviewer checking an engineering explanation needs different instructions from a clinician assessing terminology in a research summary. Identify the decision the pilot will inform, such as revising a rubric, selecting examples for further testing or assessing whether a workflow needs additional safeguards.

Keep ownership explicit. The implementation team owns the system and release decision. Expert reviewers contribute judgments within the agreed task; recruiting reviewers does not by itself establish safety, regulatory approval or legal compliance.

Translate the task into screening criteria

Start with the subfield and recent hands-on experience needed. Specify country and language when they affect the task. Explain whether current professional registration, particular equipment experience or a conflict-of-interest exclusion matters. Agree how each requirement will be checked before candidates are presented.

A broad job title is often too imprecise. For example, a hypothetical assessment of an industrial maintenance workflow might need someone with recent experience of the relevant equipment class, rather than anyone with an engineering degree. The brief should capture that difference.

Show reviewers what a useful judgment looks like

Prepare several scored examples with explanations. Include an ambiguous case and a case that cannot be judged from the available information. Define when reviewers should flag uncertainty, request clarification or escalate an issue instead of guessing.

Set a common rubric. Separate factual correctness, relevance and completeness when those dimensions matter. Specify whether reviewers may consult reference material and how they should record the basis for their assessment.

Run a small calibration exercise

Before commissioning a larger batch, give reviewers a shared set of examples. Compare their reasoning and inspect disagreements. A disagreement can reveal an unclear instruction, insufficient context or a real difference in professional judgment.

Revise the rubric where needed and preserve a record of the change. Agree who resolves disputed cases and how subsequent reviewers will receive the updated guidance.

Agree practical boundaries before recruitment

The brief should state the expected task length, session format, availability window and permitted tools. Specify what information participants may see and who approves access. Avoid including confidential or personal data in an initial recruitment enquiry; share the minimum necessary project description first.

Ask the recruitment partner to confirm feasibility, screening scope, proposed headcount, fees and timing in writing. A public service description is not confirmation that a particular specialist group is available for a particular date.

A concise brief to send

Use case and intended users.

Specialty, recent experience, country and language.

Required credential checks and exclusions.

Example task, rubric and permitted reference material.

Proposed pilot size, format and time commitment.

Deliverables, budget range and preferred timing.
